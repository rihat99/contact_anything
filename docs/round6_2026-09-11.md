## 1. Question

Does the contact head read the image (the frozen pose token; earlier, the decoder contact
tokens) or the world-space motion of the lifted body? And if the image path is worth so little,
why — the labels are not only a velocity threshold.

## 3. Reading

* Contact = static pose (0.888) + temporal motion (+0.039). The static arm beats the constant by
  0.036 and loses to a stillness threshold on hands and toes.
* Whether the velocity channels are lifted or not does not matter: the transformer sees the
  lifted body positions over time anyway.
* The frozen pose token is worth +0.005 F1 on the final recipe, all of it precision, on both
  seeds (round 5 had measured +0.001 on a weaker recipe with one seed). The decoder contact
  tokens measured the same +0.005 in round 4 (A vs F). Both are image evidence; both are at the
  reading rule's noise edge.

## 5. Conclusion given

The image path is unrewarded rather than useless: the limb labels contain no image evidence
(they are functions of pose, gravity, wall distance and stillness), the confidence weights mute
exactly the still-and-free rows where the image would decide (that is where every image-side
gain measured so far lives: still & free 0.869 vs 0.851 for tokens, 0.910 vs 0.904 for the pose
token), and the manual test labels were pre-filled from the labeler. The +0.005 of the pose
token and of the learned tokens is what survives with the signal muted. Turning the weights off
(one seed) trades recall for precision and loses 0.013 F1 — it fits the label noise, it does not
recover the image. No experiment so far bounds the image ceiling; re-annotation would.
