**C1 verdict so far:** no learned smoother lies on the fixed front. The untrained Gaussian
(σ 0.06–0.08) beats EVERY trained arm on MPJPE by 0.7–0.8 mm; training the pose-delta head with
the motion losses shrinks motion (speed ratio 0.92 → 0.83 at the same σ) and costs pose — the
same thing round 4's no-motion arm (55.36) showed. S_global stays at its initial width
(0.078 → 0.081 s). S_adaptive's jitter (22 vs 4) is the learned kernel's shape, not a
centre wander — next paragraph; the coherence arm did not touch it. J1's
trainable head overfits (test loss rising from epoch 7, train loss 1.88 vs 1.69 for the frozen
twin) and the refined body is 0.85 mm worse.

**Why the adaptive kernel jitters (`scripts/diag_adaptive_kernel.py`, 30 scenes).** The
coherence arm changed nothing (kernel-centre step 1.7 → 2.0 ms per frame, pelvis jerk 27.7 →
28.4), so the centre-wander story was wrong. Forcing the SAME S_adaptive_tok checkpoint to its
base Gaussian at test time drops the pelvis jerk 27.7 → 3.9 (joints 31.4 → 5.5; raw input 138 /
167, GT 5.2 / 12.2): the learned kernel shape itself is the jitter. Its mean root kernel over the
13 taps is a comb — `0.05 0.03 0.11 0.05 0.13 0.06 0.14 0.06 0.13 0.05 0.10 0.03 0.05` (even
offsets heavy, odd offsets light; the fixed Gaussian is `0.01 … 0.12 0.15 0.17 0.15 0.12 … 0.01`;
entropy 2.40 of ln 13 = 2.57). A comb passes frame-parity (12.5 Hz) content, and white noise
through a flat ±6-frame kernel carries 8.4× the jerk of the σ 0.10 Gaussian — the observed 7×.
The comb is there by epoch 5 (even-tap mass 0.73, then 0.72 / 0.72 at epochs 10 / 15;
untrained 0.50) and its frequency response is a WIDER low-pass than the Gaussian (1 Hz 0.71 vs
0.83, 2 Hz 0.17 vs 0.47) that passes 0.43 at 12.5 Hz (Gaussian 0.009).

Not the data: `diag_parity.py` (2026-09-08: in the trash, one-off; Welch spectra, 64-frame segments, 216 test segments;
`output_2/logs/diag_parity.log`) finds the GT has 0.4 % of its > 2 Hz power above 8 Hz and no
Nyquist excess (12.5 Hz bin = 0.29 of the > 10 Hz power, flat = 0.25; the training GT, 1 504
clips by frame spacing, 0.15–0.20), the stage-1 input is broadband (Nyquist share 0.25) and
the GT–input coherence is ≤ 0.02 in every band; F's output is 0.30, S_adaptive_tok's 0.67 —
the comb ADDS parity content that nothing in the data has. The stage-1 residual
autocorrelation (lags 1–6: 0.53, 0.24, 0.05, −0.10, −0.17, −0.15) has no structure a comb
would exploit, and the stage-1 ERROR is slow (lag-1 0.94, lag-6 0.62: bias, not noise).
`neighbours` / tap ordering verified correct.

The remaining explanation is the objective: every derivative loss on the refined body uses
central differences (`time_derivative`, `angular_velocity`, the RNEA and stillness stencils),
which are exactly blind to the Nyquist component (x[t+1] − x[t−1] = 0 for (−1)^t), and the
position losses see it as a sub-mm cost — so the kernel's parity direction is unconstrained
and the optimiser is free to widen the envelope (what the position losses want) while leaving
the Nyquist content in. `S_adaptive_tok_aligned` (tight stencils that see Nyquist) is the
test: its kernel is probed at epochs 5 / 10 / 15 (`output_2/logs/r5_kernel_aligned.log`).
**Confirmed at epoch 5**: with the tight stencils the kernel has no comb (even-tap mass 0.51;
taps `0.04 0.06 0.08 0.09 0.09 0.10 0.10 0.10 0.09 0.09 0.08 0.06 0.04`, a near-flat ±6-frame
kernel of width 0.122 s) and the pelvis jerk is 4.9 (joints 5.6) against 27.7 with the legacy
stencils at the same point of training. So the round-4 derivative losses, which every earlier
round used, cannot see a period-2 component of the output at all; any learned smoother trained
under them is free to keep it. **The finished aligned arm** lands at MPJPE 56.22 / PA 38.89 /
jitter 3.76 — F's pose exactly, 0.12 mm behind S_fixed / S_global and 0.8 mm behind the
untrained Gaussian — with the same contact (0.911) and forces (0.185 / 20.5°). The adaptive
kernel, once its loss can see what it does, is a flat ±6-frame kernel that matches but does not
beat the fixed family. C1 as stated in the plan is not met. And without any kernel the block
does not smooth by itself even with the raw − local-mean channel telling it what the noise is
(S_none_tok 57.79 / jitter 71 vs E 58.16 / 76): the convex kernel is the only thing in the
model that removes the per-frame noise, and a fixed Gaussian does it as well as anything
learned. Contacts and forces do not care which (S_none_tok 0.913 / 0.185 / 20.3°).

Reading:
* **The contact head reads the arrow of time.** Reversal leaves the pose bit-for-bit
  equivariant (56.18 vs 56.22) but costs 0.02 F1, all of it precision, 10× the seed spread.
  Either the labels are time-asymmetric (motion-gated "stable contact") or the head uses the
  approach / release velocity sign. The loss is uniform over the groups (precision hands
  −0.04 / −0.025, toes −0.03 / −0.02, heels −0.04 / −0.04; `r5_F_s42_groups.json`), i.e. an
  extra false-positive rate at every boundary rather than one limb's cue; F's onsets are
  detected better than its offsets (onset F1 0.41 vs offset 0.37 on every seed), so the labels'
  onset / offset timing is itself asymmetric and a reversed clip presents offsets as onsets.
* **Locally order-blind, globally temporal.** Content order inside 2–4 frames does not move
  contact at all; the whole clip shuffled costs 0.07–0.10 F1. The pose output is slot-bound (an
  interpolation of the neighbourhood: by-slot shuffle costs 0.1 mm) — the trained model IS a
  smoother, and E (no Gaussian) never became one.
* **Temporal attention helps contact and the motion head only.** Removing it at test time costs
  0.03 F1 and halves the acceleration correlation, while MPJPE IMPROVES by 0.37 mm (55.85):
  consistent with section 3 — the temporal block's pose delta is a net loss.
* **Half frame rate is harmless** (≤ 0.15 mm, ≤ 0.003 F1).
* The motion head's velocity flips sign under reversal (−0.74), as it should.
