# Round 10 (2026-09-13/14): limb tokens, decoder contact tokens back, gravity-aligned forces

One arm with every addition at once (the user's call: no ablations, re-run with a fix if something
misbehaves), read against a 16-epoch re-run of the round-9 recipe.

## What was built

* **Limb tokens** (`model.refiner.limb_tokens`). Seven tokens per frame instead of one: the body
  token (the assembled token of every earlier round: trajectory geometry + projected frozen pose
  token) and one token per kindyn group holding that extremity's root-frame position, its forward
  and backward one-sided body-frame rates, its crop-space position and log depth ratio to the
  pelvis, and its projected DECODER CONTACT TOKEN (the six learned tokens that ride the frozen SAM 3D
  Body decoder behind the asymmetric mask and grid-sample the image features around their MHR70
  anchor, back for the first time since round 4). One shared projection for the six limbs; the
  transformer's slot embedding tells them apart. The temporal transformer becomes VGGT-style
  **alternating** blocks (`model/rope.py::_AlternatingBlock`): RoPE attention along time inside each
  slot, then attention across the seven slots of a frame, then one FFN, all pre-LN with zero-init
  output projections (identity at init). The contact and force heads read the limb tokens (one
  logit / one 3-vector per token, shared weights); pose, gravity (and motion) still read the body
  token. The iterative feedback gets a limb twin: each limb token receives its extremity's
  recomputed geometry plus its own contact probability and force after every layer.
  Parameters: 14.8 M vs 11.3 M (the second attention per layer).
* **Gravity-aligned force frame** (`model.refiner.force_frame: gravity`). The force head's three
  numbers per limb are read in a frame whose up axis is the clip's own gravity estimate (or the
  given gravity) and whose heading is the body's forward axis projected on the horizontal plane;
  `out["force"]["frame"]` is world-from-that, so the losses, `predict_test.py`,
  `predict_reconstruction.py` and the RNEA need no change; intermediate layers' forces are rotated
  into the final layer's frame for the deep supervision. Chosen from the force anatomy below.
* **Embedding cache rebuilt** (`features/embedding`, 1.29 TB, 491,879 person-frames, bf16, built
  in 58 min on four GPUs): the decoder contact tokens need the live decoder.
* Tests: 93 (18 new; the alternating tables are checked against a per-(clip, slot) reference with
  three clips of different spacings, holes and an active window; the layer-frame transport with
  non-zero forces and distinct per-layer gravity estimates; per-head gradient reach). Two reviews
  (a fresh Opus verifier, codex gpt-6-astra): the single-token path is bit-exact against the previous
  refiner, no DDP-unused parameter under the arm's settings, gravity detached in the frame.
  `scripts/render_video.py` now draws the force arrows through `frame` (it used the root rotation).

## Force anatomy before the round (`output_4/logs/r10_force_anatomy_q{1,2,3}.log`, arm D)

* **Frames.** The GT force is near-axial on every limb (|cos| to the forearm 0.89, to the shank
  0.85) and 22-30° from vertical, but in the ROOT frame the hand directions spread over 46-98°
  (p25-p90) because the trunk leans 43-49° on the median frame. Concentration of the GT direction
  (mean resultant length): root frame 0.79 (hands) / 0.84 (toes); gravity-aligned 0.88-0.90 on every
  group; limb-aligned 0.87-0.88 hands but 0.78-0.85 feet. The gravity frame is the one frame that is
  tight for every group.
* **Error split.** 76 % of D's squared vector error is magnitude (0.122 of the 0.155 MAE); the angle
  error is a small-force effect (28° at 0.1-0.3 bw, 10° above 0.6 bw); the residual vertical bias is in
  the feet (0.87-0.89 of GT) and the heels (exactly 0).
* **Dynamics.** The predicted force is half as fast-moving as the GT (frame-to-frame RMS ratio
  0.53-0.56 hands, 0.43-0.44 toes) — smoother, not noisier; feet series correlate 0.62-0.74 against
  hands 0.78-0.91.
* **Leakage.** 65 % of the off-contact force mass sits on the 11 % of off-contact rows whose contact
  probability is above 0.5: the force follows the model's own gate, not the label.

## Reading

* The decoder contact tokens plus the per-limb token structure are worth +0.010 contact F1 and
  +0.08 transition F1 over the single-token model, all in precision and mostly on the toes — the
  image path finally pays, on the recipe where every earlier attempt (rounds 4 and 6: +0.005) did
  not. The arm cannot say how much is the image and how much the architecture (no ablation this
  round; `configs/r10/L_limb_cached.yaml` is the architecture without the image tokens, ~90 min on
  one GPU, if that question matters).
* Forces at the last checkpoint improve on every number except the angle (MAE 0.160 vs 0.165,
  off-contact 0.026 vs 0.038, angle 20.7 vs 20.4); the gravity frame makes the output more vertical
  than the GT, so the horizontal (friction) part is where the remaining direction error lives.
* The pose is a byproduct: MPJPE 52.9 (−0.8 mm) and jitter 5.5 with the same losses — the
  within-frame attention gives the body token the extremities' view.
* Checkpoint selection by contact F1 alone is no longer right: monitor a force metric too, or take
  the last epoch.
