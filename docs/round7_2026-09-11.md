## 1. Question

The final model (round 5) smooths the stage-1 body with fixed Gaussians before the temporal
transformer (root 0.10 s, rotations 0.08 s) because every learned pose correction had measured
worse. Can the transformer produce the smooth body ITSELF — pose offsets on the root position,
the root rotation and the 21 joint rotations — with lifted jitter at the GT floor (6.9 in GVHMR
units of 10 m/s^3) and MPJPE not worse than the Gaussian body (55.5 mm), under derivative losses
that HELP instead of shrinking the motion?

## 2. Root cause of "the block never filtered"

1. **Every earlier derivative loss and the token's own velocity channels were central
   differences.** A central first difference has exactly zero response to a period-2
   (Nyquist) alternation, and that is the component the jitter metric (a third difference)
   weighs most. Replacing them with one-sided forward / backward differences (loss stencil
   `forward`, token `one_sided_velocity`) took the root-position jitter from 90 to ~12 and the
   root-rotation jitter from 36 to ~8.5 within two epochs.
2. **The articulation had only absolute rotation channels.** Filtering an absolute channel
   through a residual block needs "neighbours minus own", which is badly conditioned; a rate
   channel makes the correction a signed sum of the neighbours' rates (telescoping to
   `p[t+k] - p[t]`: an anchor plus a local correction, no integration, no drift). Per-joint
   one-sided parent-local angular rates (`token.joint_velocity`, +126 channels) took the
   articulation jitter from ~23 to ~8.7.
3. **A pointwise acceleration loss against the raw GT is the shrink trap** (target pearson
   0.14; arm A: joint velocity ratio 0.81, MPJPE 63), and matching the raw GT's acceleration
   AMPLITUDE injects its noise (arm E: MPJPE 75-81). A pointwise VELOCITY loss against the raw
   GT works (raw velocity is 90 % predictable). Position losses alone do not smooth at all
   (arm H: jitter 70 flat) — the velocity loss is what drives the smoothing, the rate channels
   only make it learnable.
4. **Per-layer iterative refinement closes the last gap** (`model.refiner.iterative`, the
   user's SAM3D-decoder analogy): the shared zero-init head runs after every layer, deltas
   accumulate, FK per layer, and the corrected trajectory's own root-frame joints and one-sided
   rates are fed back into the residual stream (LayerNorm + zero-init Linear). Without it the
   later layers keep reading the RAW trajectory's rates. Jitter 10.5 -> 6.7.

Two ceilings measured on the way: an LTI (Wiener) filter of the stage-1 body caps at 55.4 mm =
the Gaussian, and 82 % of the root error power is below 0.5 Hz (stage-1 bias, not temporal
noise); the jitter is a free parameter of the position objective (2.6-17 at equal MPJPE).

## 3. Code built

* `model/loss/motion.py`: `stencil: forward`; the motion-head terms are optional (no `motion`
  output needed); `loss.pose_<q>_rms` amplitude terms (built for arm E, keep off);
  `layer_weight` deep supervision of the intermediate layers (with `smplx_supervision`);
  metric-only `pose_s_*` (GT smoothed 0.12 s, prediction raw) and `pose_ss_*` (both smoothed:
  the band-limited comparison, `amp_ratio` < 1 there is real attenuation); the `pose_ss_<q>`
  weights make the band-limited comparison a LOSS.
* `model/loss/reference.py`: `GaussianReferenceLoss`, an annealed distance to the Gaussian body
  (`Loss.progress` set by the trainer). Measured useless (arm B), kept as a tool.
* `model/refiner.py`: `token.one_sided_velocity`, `token.joint_velocity`, `iterative`,
  `feedback_delta`, per-layer outputs (`*_layers`); `model/rope.py` `prepare` / `run_block`.
* `model/loss/smplx.py`: eval diagnostics `jitter_root_only` / `rot_only` / `artic_only` (GT
  swap), `vel_ratio_root` / `rot` / `joints`, `root_err_hf` / `lf`.
* `optim.head_lr_scale` (a trainable stage-1 head); `scripts/predict_test.py --checkpoint none`.
* Tests: `tests/test_refiner.py` (+ the one-sided stencils, iterative identity, band-limited
  gradient), `tests/test_metrics_diag.py`.

## 6. Reading

* The temporal block CAN replace the Gaussians once it can see the Nyquist band (one-sided
  stencils on both sides) and has rate channels for every quantity it must filter. The result
  is a body at the GT jitter floor that is 0.6 mm better than the Gaussian body in MPJPE and
  0.45 mm in PA-MPJPE (small; five times the seed spread), with better in-band acceleration.
* Iterative refinement (I) is what reaches the floor; delta feedback (J), deep supervision (K)
  and a fourth layer (M) are equivalent on pose. K is the recipe to keep: the lowest jitter and
  the fastest convergence.
* Every iterative arm reaches its jitter minimum around epoch 20-25 of 60 (epoch 15 of 30 in
  P) and then drifts up by 1-2 units while MPJPE improves by hundredths: the drift follows the
  cosine position, so `best.pth` (min jitter) is the checkpoint to use.
* Nothing moves the pelvis error (110 mm; Gaussian 117; stage 1 120): it is the stage-1
  low-frequency bias, and a trainable stage-1 head (L) makes it worse.
* Acceleration losses: pointwise on raw targets shrinks (A), amplitude matching injects noise
  (E), smoothed targets shrink the velocity (N). The band-limited formulation (O) is the only
  one with no shrink direction by construction.

## 7. Conclusion given

The recipe to keep is `configs/smooth/P_k30.yaml` (K on 30 epochs): iterative refinement with
deep supervision, one-sided rate channels for the root and every joint, the pointwise forward-
difference velocity losses on the raw GT (no acceleration term), no input Gaussian, `best.pth`
(minimum jitter, ~epoch 15). Against the Gaussian body it is 0.6 mm better in MPJPE and 0.45 mm
in PA-MPJPE (paired intervals exclude zero; five times the seed spread), at the GT jitter floor
(5.9 vs the floor 6.9 vs the Gaussian's over-smoothed 2.7), with the pelvis error 110 vs 117 mm
and a better in-band acceleration (0.77 vs 0.70, angular 0.91 vs 0.86); seed 1 replicates it
(54.89 / 6.18, paired +0.58). What did not help: the Gaussian as a reference (B, G), every acceleration term
(A, E, N, O), a trainable stage-1 head (L), a fourth layer (M), delta feedback (J). What is not
solved: the 110 mm pelvis error is stage-1 low-frequency bias (82 % of the root error power
below 0.5 Hz) that no temporal model can remove.
